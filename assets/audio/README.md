# Original music and procedural sound effects

The twenty-two WAV files and nine Ogg music layers in this folder are synthesized from mathematical oscillators,
envelopes, and deterministic pseudorandom noise. No recorded samples, downloaded
sound packs, pretrained audio-generation model, or paid services are used.

Implementation and asset production: AI-assisted work by OpenAI Codex for
SCRAPSTORM: NIGHT SHIFT, 2026-09-07. No human composer or performer is claimed.

Rebuild sound effects with Python 3.11+ and its standard library:

```text
python tools/generate_audio.py
```

For music synthesis and waveform validation only, install the pinned optional
packages from the project root: `python -m pip install -r requirements-audio.txt`.
Use a virtual environment; these are not needed to play, export, or run the core
Godot regressions. Then `python tools/validate_audio.py` measures the delivered
files without changing them. The pinned environment is NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1,
SoundFile 0.12.1, CFFI 2.0.0, and pycparser 3.0. Generator runs replace asset outputs;
do not regenerate assets just to build. Ogg encoder/platform differences may
change regenerated bytes; manifests identify the actual delivered files.

The editable synthesis source is `tools/generate_audio.py`. Output is mono,
22,050 Hz, signed 16-bit PCM WAV with a peak at approximately -3.7 dBFS before
the game's additional event gains. All sounds are shorter than one second.

Foam and arc play once per actual automatic attack, both at -17 dB event gain
with 0.30 and 0.25 second minimum intervals. They only use idle voices in the
eight-voice pool and never replace playing UI or combat feedback. The v0.4 additions
preserve all fourteen v0.3 WAV files byte for byte by appending to the existing
seed order. The nine music layers are unchanged. `sfx_manifest.json` records the
22 delivered WAV hashes, seeds, frame counts, peak, RMS and finite-window mean.

Recovery and enemy-down cues are quieter than the early prototype, at -16 and
-13 dB event gain. Recovery, enemy-down, foam, arc and discharge cycle through five
subtle playback-pitch variants (0.965–1.045), independent of gameplay randomness.
Warning and charge cues keep a fixed pitch and receive priority over automatic
tool events. The mixer never creates more than eight effect players. When saturated, important
cues replace a lower-priority voice first; skill, reward and installation cues
cannot replace a playing safety warning or boss-charge cue. Automatic effects
only take idle voices. Dedicated event gains and minimum intervals are defined
in `scripts/services/sound_bank.gd`; new cues keep fixed pitch for recognition.

## Three original adaptive scores

These arrangements were composed for v0.3 and retained in v0.4; they are not edits of the old short cues or
melodies from downloaded music. Every score contains 32 bars and four 8-bar
sections: statement, drive, breakdown and final lift. There are chord changes,
melodic call/answer phrases, variations in note register, bass patterns, drum
fills, stereo delay and different instrumental densities. A reduced-percussion
breakdown and denser final section make each cycle more than a repeated short riff.

| Map | Score | Style and instrumentation | Tempo | Loop duration | Seed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yard | Afterhours Salvage | D-minor industrial breakbeat; syncopated kick, FM bell phrases, metal accents, plucked bass and soft chord pad | 112 BPM | 68.5714 s | 270901 |
| foundry | Pressure Circuit | F-sharp-minor mechanical techno; four-on-the-floor kick, offbeat pulse bass, lead replies and machine percussion | 128 BPM | 60.0000 s | 270902 |
| reactor | Neon Induction | C-minor synthwave/electro; stereo chord pad, upper-register lead, arpeggios and syncopated electro rhythm | 104 BPM | 73.8462 s | 270903 |

Each map has phase-aligned `bed`, `rhythm` and `boss` Ogg Vorbis files in `music/`.
The bed contains melody and harmony, rhythm adds drums and bass, and boss adds a
pitched ostinato, toms and metal accents. All layers are stereo, 32,000 Hz, with
matching frame counts within a map. Note tails, delays and room reflections wrap
around the loop instead of being cut off. The nine compressed files total about
5.0 MiB. Ogg import flags and runtime streams enable looping.

Rebuild with Python, NumPy, SciPy and SoundFile (free libraries):

```text
python tools/generate_music.py
python tools/validate_audio.py
```

SoundFile writes Vorbis in bounded blocks to avoid a native Windows encoder stack
overflow. The generator uses deterministic musical events and noise seeds; Ogg
container bytes can change across encoder versions, so the actual delivered-file
hashes, frame counts and measurements are captured in `music/manifest.json`.

| Map | Bed RMS | Rhythm RMS | Boss-layer RMS | Combined decoded peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yard | -24.085 dBFS | -23.638 dBFS | -26.252 dBFS | -2.111 dBFS |
| foundry | -24.516 dBFS | -22.918 dBFS | -25.858 dBFS | -1.951 dBFS |
| reactor | -22.550 dBFS | -23.520 dBFS | -27.418 dBFS | -2.070 dBFS |

RMS dBFS measures signal energy; it is **not integrated LUFS**, speaker loudness,
or a claim of human listening approval. Detailed per-section energy, event counts,
loop boundary steps, DC offsets, SHA-256 and source paths are in the manifest.

## Runtime integration and mix

Call `SoundBank.set_context(map_id, phase)` with map `yard`, `foundry` or `reactor`
and phase `menu`, `combat`, `shop`, `boss`, `pause` or `result`. Repeated contexts
are idempotent. Phase changes interpolate layer gains without restarting their
playback clocks. A map change crossfades between two fixed three-player banks
over 1.5 seconds; the old bank stops after fading out. This is at most six music
players and eight SFX players. Menu/result use a quiet melodic bed, shop retains
soft percussion, pause removes percussion, and boss introduces its extra layer.

`Settings.music_volume` and `Settings.sfx_volume` are independent multipliers;
`Settings.master_volume` remains the overall volume. The game applies additional
music headroom so the discharge, hit and warning cues stay prominent. Native
startup fades into the quiet menu; Web waits for an actual pressed key or mouse
button, with mouse motion insufficient to unlock playback. Music does not restart
every frame, on pause, on settings changes or when purchasing an upgrade.

`SoundBank.get_music_state()` exposes context and bounded-player diagnostics for
integration checks. v0.4 adds `skill_pulse`, `skill_singularity`, `skill_overdrive`,
`singularity_burst`, `skill_ready`, `boss_break`, `evolve` and `install`.

## Verification and listening limits

`tools/validate_audio.py`: **170 checks passed**: 112 for the 22 WAV effects and
58 for the nine delivered Ogg layers. WAV checks cover the exact inventory,
hashes, PCM format, complete lengths, nonempty finite samples, headroom, attack/tail
endpoints and unique files; Ogg checks retain loop joins, low DC, stereo stem
alignment, real section variation and distinct map scores. Short one-shot sample
means are reported rather than treated as long-loop DC measurements. All WAV
peaks remain approximately 0.65 before runtime event gain.

Results are in `artifacts/audio-validation.json`; `artifacts/v04-audio-preservation.json`
confirms all fourteen existing WAV and nine Ogg files retain their exact hashes.
Three full combat mix auditions in `artifacts/audio-preview/` are review artifacts,
not game distribution assets.

`tests/test_audio.gd`: **69 checks passed**, covering all resources, loop flags,
bounded players, gesture gating, idempotent contexts, fade/duck/boss transitions,
independent volume, all eight new cues, saturated-pool allocation and shutdown.
The new priority checks exercise allocation without starting playback threads.
Headless runs deliberately do not start the dummy audio driver's playback threads.

These checks do not establish that the music is enjoyable, that warnings can be
heard clearly on every speaker, or that Web audio has been audibly verified.
Actual listening and the creator's feedback remain separate acceptance steps.

Native exit regression: `tools/probe_audio_exit.gd` starts three real Ogg playback
layers and then awaits `SoundBank.shutdown()` before quitting. The shutdown stops
and releases streams, blocks new cues/contexts, and waits 180 ms plus a frame for
the audio mix thread to retire playback objects. Immediate stop-and-quit reproduced
12 leaked objects/6 retained resources; awaited shutdown and the three-map native
capture fixture exited without errors or leaked resources. Automated capture
callers should await this method instead of quitting immediately after `stop_all`.
